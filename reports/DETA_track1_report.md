**Abstract.** Row 1 of our 10-row submission for proband PROBAND01 is BUB1B c.2210T>G p.(Leu737Ter) with c.3006T>G p.(Asn1002Lys) (GRCh38 chr15:40209701 T>G and chr15:40220612 T>G), submitted with moderate confidence. Candidates were ranked by four rule versions, each hashed (sha256) before it ran and logged in a hypothesis register of 101 append-only entries; BUB1B is rank 1 under rules v3 and v4. The frozen rules were then tested out of sample by writing public ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic alleles into the child's own background: 1,611 ranking trials across eight pre-registered strata, plus a second pre-registered spike-in of 779 trials that chose the row-building rule (mean rank points 80.2 against 49.1 for our draft rule). What the ranking achieves is conditional, and the condition is measurable. In recessive genes outside the primary set that match at least one of the child's phenotype terms, rank-1 recall is 0.857 [0.814, 0.893] over 300 trials; in genes matching none it is 0.010 [0.003, 0.026] over 300. The same split holds inside the primary stratum, as a descriptive stratification that was not the pre-registered test: 160 of 163 trials (0.982) in phenotype-matched genes inside the ranking universe, and 0 of 121 in genes matching no term, every one of which landed at rank 2. The pre-registered test was the mixture of those two populations and its bar was not met: rank-1 recall 0.533 [0.477, 0.589] across the 14 of 18 MVA and phenocopy genes that held an eligible allele, against a pre-registered bar of 0.80, with a gene-clustered interval of [0.27, 0.80] and top-10 recall 0.947. Rank-1 recall for other BUB1B pairs is 1.000 over 100 trials. Four limits qualify all of this. Rank 1 depends on the ClinVar evidence tier: with ClinVar hidden no novel causal reaches rank 1 (0.000), although 0.790 reach the top 10. One MVA gene (RNU4ATAC) carries the highest phenotype score in the set and is never ranked at all, because it is non-coding and invisible to an exonic pipeline. Phase is not measured from short reads, so the compound-heterozygous configuration is a hypothesis. And the three calibrated variant-effect models disagree on p.Asn1002Lys. The EPCR values are calibrated from the spike-in.

## 4. Methods

Design. Every test was written into a hypothesis register, with a falsifier, before it ran, and its outcome was recorded as ruled out, completed without a result, or not testable. Results were appended, never edited. Ranking, calibration and spike-in rules were hashed (sha256) before they ran; revisions are dated with their reasons.

Data. Organizer dataset SageBio/mva-hackathon-2026-data at commit 59e322d (a later commit changed only the README): Sentieon GRCh38 VCF (5,012,204 records; sha256 bec64c68...42175, equal to the Hugging Face stored hash), clinical document, FASTQ. We aligned the reads ourselves (PROBAND01.bam) for read-level checks.

Exonic frequency pass. All 87,198 non-reference calls in protein-coding exons (GENCODE v44 basic, +/-3 bp) were classified by gnomAD v4.1 exome frequency from regional reads of the public files (no child coordinates sent): common or low-frequency 71,416, rare 839, unobserved 14,943. Consequence was computed from the reference on 19,690 canonical transcripts. After a bug fix (section 8), 481 distinct protein-altering calls are rare or unobserved on the any-transcript definition (485 call-transcript rows, 3 of them chrM and excluded); the same table gives 478 taking the first canonical transcript per call, which is the definition section 8 uses for the withdrawn earlier figure.

Paralog-family exclusion, declared in the frozen rule before the run and carried unchanged through v2 to v4: gene symbols beginning HLA-, TAS2R, MUC, LILRA, LILRB, OR followed by a digit, ZNF, PRDM9, NBPF or SERPINA are reported separately and are not ranked. Under v1 the exclusion removed 189 candidate calls in 39 genes and left 56 genes ranked; once the v2 male-X and cluster filters are added, 277 candidate calls reach the ranking. The genome-wide compound-heterozygous pattern (a rare loss-of-function-type call and a rare missense call in the same gene, phase unknown) does not single out BUB1B: 13 genes carry the pattern on the pre-fix table and the same 13 carry it on the corrected table, so 12 genes besides BUB1B show it, most of them in paralogous or highly variable families.

Ranking rules, in the order written:
- v1: weighted consequence score. BUB1B rank 8; a shuffled-label baseline put it at 7. Flaws recorded: chrX treated as diploid in a male child; an artifact cluster.
- v2: male-X and cluster filters. BUB1B rank 6; shuffled baseline 4. Data-only weighting does not separate BUB1B from background.
- v3: evidence tiers. A = a call classified Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic in ClinVar; B = loss-of-function-type plus missense calls in one gene; C = other genes passing v2. BUB1B rank 1. Without the ClinVar tier it is rank 6, and with ClinVar labels shuffled it leaves tier A. Rank 1 therefore rests on existing knowledge.
- v4: within each tier, order by the number of the child's 8 HPO terms matched to the gene's HPO annotations through is_a ancestry (HPO release 2026-09-01). Amendment 1 (dated, before the corrected run) excludes chrM calls. BUB1B rank 1; only 3 of 52 candidate genes match any term. The recorded v4 hash covers the rule text as first frozen; Amendment 1 was appended afterwards, dated and before the corrected run, and the amended file's hash is recorded beside it.
- The 8 HPO IDs in the rule (HP:0000121, HP:0001508, HP:0001518, HP:0001622, HP:0002859, HP:0003202, HP:0004322, HP:0200067) were checked against the organizers' clinical document: exact match, labels equal to hp.obo names.
- Script defects found and handled without changing any rule: the rerun script for v4 on the corrected table had dropped the Amendment 1 chrM line (three MT- genes appeared at ranks 7, 9, 10); the submission applies the amendment as written, which moves no other row. The frozen v4b script reads the pre-fix v3 output; applied to the fixed v3 output it gives the same top 10.

## 5. Phase (P1): unresolved

Read-backed phasing was pre-registered with a fixed rule (a chain of heterozygous sites, at least 5 bridging read pairs per link, at least 90% concordance). One heterozygous SNV lies between the two calls, leaving gaps of at least 6,769 bp and 4,142 bp. Of 2,029 read-1 templates in the window, the largest insert is 1,590 bp, so no fragment spans either gap and no chain can be built: **UNRESOLVED**. The limit is the short-read fragment length.

Deviation: the script that produced this result checks fragment span against gap length only; it does not count allele-carrying read pairs at base quality 20 and has no proper-pair filter, unlike the pre-registered text. The result does not change, because zero fragments span either gap under any allele-level filter.

Resolving phase needs long reads spanning about 11 kb or parental genotypes; the dataset has neither. Row 1 is therefore a compound-heterozygous hypothesis. The scorer treats a row as an unordered variant set and does not use phase.

## 6. Out-of-sample test: spike-in of known causal events

Question: does the frozen ranking put known causal events at rank 1, or did it work only for this child? The test was pre-registered and hashed before any spiked ranking ran (20 October), then audited independently (83 of 83 rebuilt trials matched).

Design: the child's two BUB1B rows were removed; public ClinVar Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic alleles were written into the child's own background as biallelic genotypes; the frozen consequence step and rules v3 and v4 were rerun per trial. This tests ranking only, not read-level calling or phasing, and the child's eight phenotype terms are held fixed across all 1,611 trials, so the test cannot detect overfitting to that phenotype vector.

Findings:
- **Negative robustness result.** The pre-registered bar was rank-1 recall of at least 0.80; we measured 0.533, so the bar was not met, and the hypothesis register records that outcome under its own label, FALSIFIED. (The same pre-registration predicted 0.4 to 0.6 before the run.) Four of the 18 genes in the set (RNU6ATAC, SNRNP48, CEP192, MAD2L1BP) held no eligible public Pathogenic/Likely pathogenic allele to write in and contributed no trial, so the estimate is over the 14 genes that could be sampled. Five MVA genes (CDC20, CENATAC, CENPF, SLF2, SMC5) never reach rank 1 because HPO does not annotate them with any of the child's terms; the non-coding RNU4ATAC is invisible to the exonic pipeline.
- **Gene clustering.** Outcomes are nearly fixed per gene. Resampling genes rather than trials gives 0.533 with a 95% interval of [0.27, 0.80]; the trial-level intervals hold only for this gene mix.
- **ClinVar dependence.** With ClinVar hidden, no novel causal reaches rank 1 (0 of 300), although 79% reach the top 10. Rank 1 in this pipeline needs the ClinVar tier.
- **Control blocking.** Unrelated recessive genes reach rank 1 in 3 of 300 trials, all through ClinVar records shared with an overlapping phenotype-matched gene (0 of 292 otherwise). Without the phenotype layer (rule v3) they reach rank 1 in 76%. The phenotype layer gains this specificity at the cost of MVA-set recall (v4 0.533 vs v3 0.840).
- **BUB1B specifically.** Other BUB1B P/LP pairs rank 1 in 100 of 100 trials, so the child's rank 1 does not depend on these exact alleles.
- **Stop-loss blind spot.** The consequence classifier reads only GENCODE exon and CDS features and ignores the stop_codon feature, so a stop-codon change is labelled UTR and is never scored as stop-loss (0 of 311). A stop-loss causal variant would be missed, or would receive at most half credit through a partner.

What determines rank 1 in this design, stated descriptively. Two pre-registered strata point at the phenotype layer: recessive genes outside the MVA set that match at least one of the child's HPO terms reach rank 1 in 0.857 [0.814, 0.893] of 300 trials, and genes that match none reach it in 0.010 [0.003, 0.026] of 300. Those two strata are different gene sets, so they differ in gene membership as well as in phenotype match, and with outcomes nearly fixed per gene that contrast is suggestive, not a controlled single-variable result. The controlled comparison is the v3 against v4 ablation, which changes one rule inside the same genes and the same trials: the phenotype layer costs recall in the primary stratum, 0.840 under v3 against 0.533 under v4, and buys specificity in the control stratum, where unrelated genes reach rank 1 in 76% of trials under v3 against 1% under v4. The same split runs through the primary stratum. Taking the per-gene outcomes and grouping them by phenotype score, the eight genes with a score of at least 1 that are inside the ranking universe give 160 rank-1 hits in 163 trials (0.982), and the five genes with a score of 0 give 0 in 121, every one of them landing at rank 2 behind LZTR1. RNU4ATAC is counted in neither group and is reported on its own: it carries the highest phenotype score of any gene in the set, 4, and was never ranked in any of its 16 trials, because it is non-coding and an exonic pipeline cannot see it. All 160 rank-1 hits in the primary stratum come from the phenotype-matched group, so the pre-registered 0.533 is the mixture of those two populations and nothing else. The grouping reconstructs the stratum's published distribution exactly, which is the check that it is a real division and not a convenient slice: 163 + 121 + 16 = 300 trials, and 160 rank-1, 124 rank-2 and 16 never ranked = 300 outcomes, with the 124 at rank 2 resolving as the 121 phenotype-score-zero trials plus three TRIM37 misses. **This grouping is descriptive and was not the pre-registered test. It does not rescue the bar, which was not met on the stratum the pre-registration named.** What it establishes is the condition under which the rule reaches rank 1, and that the condition is measurable before the rule is run: it is whether HPO annotates the gene with a term the child has.

EPCR. Row r gets the smoothed fraction of primary-stratum trials whose causal landed at rank r, (k + 0.5)/301, made monotone, with a 1e-5 step per row to keep values distinct (frozen rule). Row 1: 160/300 gives 0.533223. Row 2: 124/300 gives 0.413611. Rows 3 to 10: 0/300 each, giving 0.001641 down to 0.001571.
- EPCR is a calibration of the ranking, not a posterior for this child. For BUB1B events the measured rank-1 rate is 1.000, so 0.533 is conservative for row 1.
- **EPCR at row 2 measures "the slot behind the top phenotype-matched gene", not LZTR1's own probability of being causal.** In the masked background the rank-2 mass is MVA genes with no HPO match sitting behind LZTR1.
- The scorer sorts by EPCR and sweeps EPCR thresholds; with strictly decreasing distinct values only the order matters.

Row-building rule (PRE-REG-ROWMAP). The draft rule used before this test paired, in any gene with more than two candidate calls, the highest-weight loss-of-function-type call with the lowest-position missense call. A second spike-in, pre-registered and hashed before it ran (sha256 9f7a440d..., frozen 2026-10-20 11:31:36Z; first trial ranking 11:37:14Z), compared that draft rule with five mechanical alternatives under three row constructions. Each trial wrote a known compound-heterozygous causal pair and one or two decoy heterozygous calls (public ClinVar alleles not classified P/LP) into the causal gene in the child's background, and every trial file was scored with the organizers' unmodified `evaluation.py`.

- The difference in mean rank points is +31.1 (95% stratified gene-cluster bootstrap interval [26.5, 35.5]; the pre-registered Bonferroni gate interval for 17 comparisons, [24.3, 37.7], excludes zero), so the pre-registered decision rule adopted P5 with construction C3. One deviation, in a sanity-check implementation, affected no decision.
- Near-tie (post hoc): the adopted rule beats two other mechanical rules (the top two calls by score, P2, and the weaker-allele rule, P3) by only +0.13 points [0.03, 0.26], and a fixed filter rule (P1) by +1.32 [-1.36, 3.77]. Almost all of the gain comes from dropping our draft rule, not from the choice among its replacements.
- Why the draft rule failed (post hoc diagnostic): it always paired one loss-of-function-type call with a missense call, so a missense decoy took the place of a second truncating or splice-region causal allele. In the 444 trials with such a causal pair it built the correct pair in none (0 of 444).
