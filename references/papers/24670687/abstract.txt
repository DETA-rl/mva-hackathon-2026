1. Sci Transl Med. 2014 Mar 26;6(229):229ra43. doi: 10.1126/scitranslmed.3007965.

Cytotoxicity of paclitaxel in breast cancer is due to chromosome missegregation 
on multipolar spindles.

Zasadil LM(1), Andersen KA, Yeum D, Rocque GB, Wilke LG, Tevaarwerk AJ, Raines 
RT, Burkard ME, Weaver BA.

Author information:
(1)Department of Cell and Regenerative Biology, University of Wisconsin, 
Madison, WI 53705, USA.

The blockbuster chemotherapy drug paclitaxel is widely presumed to cause cell 
death in tumors as a consequence of mitotic arrest, as it does at concentrations 
routinely used in cell culture. However, we determine here that paclitaxel 
levels in primary breast tumors are well below those required to elicit 
sustained mitotic arrest. Instead, cells in these lower concentrations of drug 
proceed through mitosis without substantial delay and divide their chromosomes 
on multipolar spindles, resulting in chromosome missegregation and cell death. 
Consistent with these cell culture data, most mitotic cells in primary human 
breast cancers contain multipolar spindles after paclitaxel treatment. Contrary 
to the previous hypothesis, we find that mitotic arrest is dispensable for tumor 
regression in patients. These results demonstrate that mitotic arrest is not 
responsible for the efficacy of paclitaxel, which occurs because of chromosome 
missegregation on highly abnormal, multipolar spindles. This mechanistic insight 
may be used to improve selection of future antimitotic drugs and to identify a 
biomarker with which to select patients likely to benefit from paclitaxel.
